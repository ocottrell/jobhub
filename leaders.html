Role of the Minister

How to walk alongside someone who is looking for work.

Leadership — about 2 minutes.

Role of the minister — about 2½ minutes.

In all your efforts, begin with a prayer.

Become familiar with the website, and help those you support work through it.

Review the Church's employment services site.

The four things you do

1 Understand what they need
  • Learn their skills, experience and where they want to go.assessment sheet to come
  • Find what is standing in the way — missing skills, gaps in work history, or confidence.
  • Ask about immediate needs: money, transportation, childcare.
2 Keep their spirits up
  • Check in regularly. Encouragement and accountability are the same act.
  • Help them set goals that are realistic, with dates attached.
  • Give them somewhere safe to say the discouraging part out loud.
3 Hold them to it
  • Ask how much support they actually want. Then give that much.
  • If it fits, set a standing time to meet.agenda to come
  • Set goals together, write them down, and follow up on them.
  • Encourage them to fill out the Roles document.link to come
4 Stay with them after
  • Keep in touch once they are hired. The first months decide whether it lasts.
  • Offer advice as they look toward what comes next.
  • Help them think past this job to the one after it.

Things to consider

• Offer encouragement and support
  • Listen actively. Let them say what is hard without hearing judgement back.
  • Pray with them. Remind them, out loud, of their worth.
  • Build confidence. Name the specific gifts you see. General praise does not land; specific does.
• Connect them with resources
  • Job boards. Share local boards, career fairs and hiring events.
  • Community networks. Use what the congregation knows. Most jobs still come through people.
  • Workshops. Point them to resume help, interview practice and training courses.
• Use what the congregation has
  • Start a job support group. People searching together carry it better than people searching alone.
  • Ask those with expertise. Anyone who has hired people can teach someone to be hired.
  • Keep a job board. Physical or online — somewhere leads can be posted.
• Help with the practical parts
  • Resumes and applications. Offer to read them. A second pair of eyes finds what they cannot see.
  • Mock interviews. Practising out loud is the single best preparation there is.
  • Rides and childcare. Interviews are lost over transportation more often than over qualifications.
• Tend to the spirit
  • Encourage patience. Philippians 4:13 and Proverbs 3:5–6 both speak to this.
  • Push back on shame. Being out of work is a circumstance, not a verdict on a person.
  • Offer counsel. Where stress or discouragement runs deep, help them find real support.
• Work with local organisations
  • Nonprofits. Local workforce development groups do this full time.
  • Assistance programs. Government programs, food banks and financial aid ease the pressure while they search.
